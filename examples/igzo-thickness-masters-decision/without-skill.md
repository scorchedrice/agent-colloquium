## Direct answer

Yes—this could become a good master's thesis, but the current idea needs a sharper research question.

The remembered observation is scientifically plausible: IGZO TFT performance can have an optimum channel thickness because very thin films may be strongly affected by interface defects and incomplete coverage, while thicker films may experience bulk defects, poorer gate control, or higher off-current. The answer reports prior optima of about [58 nm](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0749603613002620) and [130 nm](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.surfcoat.2010.07.036), depending on deposition and device conditions.

It argues that “find the best thickness” alone may be too incremental. Its stronger thesis question is:

> What limits the minimum practical IGZO channel thickness, and how can the deposition or post-treatment process shift that limit while preserving mobility and switching performance?

It proposes a feasible master's project:

1. Fabricate IGZO TFTs with thicknesses from 3–50 nm.
2. Control composition, substrate, gate dielectric, electrode structure, annealing, and oxygen partial pressure.
3. Measure mobility, threshold voltage, subthreshold swing, on/off ratio, hysteresis, leakage, and bias-stress stability.
4. Determine whether degradation is driven by interface traps, oxygen-vacancy concentration, incomplete or rough film coverage, contact resistance, or bulk defects.
5. Use oxygen pressure, annealing, passivation, or a thin interfacial layer to shift the optimum toward a thinner channel.

The answer treats oxygen concentration as a controlled variable and cites an [IEEE study](https://doi.org/10.1109/TED.2018.2837084) that it says observed mobility falling from 12 to 0.1 cm²/V·s as oxygen pressure increased. It also warns that contact resistance and threshold-voltage shifts can make field-effect mobility appear worse at small thicknesses, suggesting transmission-line measurements or multiple channel lengths where possible.

It proposes the title *Process Engineering of Ultrathin IGZO Thin-Film Transistors for Reduced Channel Thickness* and a success criterion of reducing the minimum channel thickness by 30–50% while retaining at least 90% of the reference mobility, with acceptable leakage, subthreshold swing, and bias stability.

Its conclusion is that the direction is suitable for a master's degree when framed around the mechanism and process window for reducing thickness—not merely mobility-versus-thickness optimization.
